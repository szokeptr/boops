# Calling a bot

You can talk to a bot out loud. The bot answers in its own voice, and it can still use its tools, files and notes while you talk.

## Start a call

Open a conversation with the bot and click the phone button next to its name. You can also open the command palette and choose **Call <bot name>**. The call stays in a small panel while you use the rest of the app. Minimize it to a pill, mute your microphone, or hang up from there. The bot says goodbye when you do, but you hang up yourself.

On iPhone, calls use the system call screen, so they keep going with the screen locked and work with AirPods and CarPlay.

## Set up

Calls run on OpenAI's GPT-Live voice model and are billed to your own OpenAI API key, at about $0.05 a minute. Add the key in **Settings → Providers → Voice calls** on the machine that runs the bot. Until you add one, the call screen offers to take you there.

## Voice and language

Each bot keeps its own voice. To change it, open the bot's settings on any device and pick a **Call voice**. Most voices adapt to any language; the others are made for one language and accent. **Call language** holds the bot to one language. Left at **Match yours**, it speaks whatever language you speak. Changes apply from the next call.

## What the bot remembers

- When you ask for something that needs real work, the bot sends the request into the conversation. It appears there as your own words, marked as said on a call. The bot tells you the result when it is ready, and the reply also stays in the chat.
- When you hang up, the call's transcript is added to the conversation. The bot reads it and saves anything worth remembering.
- Only the bot's owner can call it. People you share a bot with cannot.
