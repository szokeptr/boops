# Group chats

A group chat puts you, the people you invited, and your bots in one conversation. Bots answer when someone mentions them, and can chime in on their own when the chat is about something they can help with.

## Start one

Click **+** next to **Group chats** in the sidebar, or open the command palette and choose **New group chat**. Pick the bots and people, and give it a name or leave the one Boops suggests. The first time, Boops also asks what everyone should call you; you can change it later in **Settings → Members → Group chats**.

People you invited can start group chats too, once you turn on **Can start group chats** for them in **Settings → Members**. They can bring only the bots you shared with them.

## Talk to a bot

Type `@` and pick a bot, or write its name after `@`. Every bot you mention answers. Each one reads what was said since it last spoke, so you can talk among yourselves first and bring a bot in when you need it. A bot you add to a chat catches up on its last 20 messages.

## Bots chiming in

With an OpenAI API key in **Settings → Providers**, bots can also speak up without a mention. A few seconds after people stop typing, a small OpenAI model reads the recent messages and picks the one bot they are most likely for, if any. Usually that is the bot that just answered, when you follow up on what it said. The bot then decides for itself whether it has something to add, and stays quiet otherwise. Mentioning a bot keeps the others out of it. Without a key, bots answer only when mentioned.

Every bot chimes in by default. To change that, or to say what a bot is there for, such as "flights and hotels", choose **Bots chiming in** from the chat's menu. Whoever started the chat, and you as owner, can change it.

Bots can mention each other to hand work along. After a few hand-offs they stop and wait for a person.

Anyone in the chat can stop a bot that is working: clear the message box and press stop, then pick the bot if several are answering. That also cancels what it was asked to do next.

Attach photos and files with the paperclip, by pasting, or by dropping them on the message box. A bot you mention gets the files sent since it last spoke.

## Approvals and questions

When a bot needs a go-ahead or an answer, a card appears above the message box. The person whose message the bot is working on answers it; you can always answer it too. Everyone else sees who it is waiting on. An approval in a group chat covers that one action; there is no "always allow", since the bot's next turn may be for someone else.

## What bots share

A bot answers a group chat with what it knows: its notes, its files, and the Connections you shared with members. It doesn't repeat your private conversations with it. While anyone you invited is in the chat, the bot uses only the Connections you shared with members. Work a bot does for someone runs at that person's access.

## Notifications

Every message notifies everyone else in the chat on their phones, and in the desktop and web app with your usual notification settings. Mute a chat from its menu, or right-click it in the sidebar, to silence it. A bot waiting on your answer still notifies you in a muted chat. In the desktop and web app, bots handing work along notify you once, when the last one answers.

## Leave, archive or delete

Anyone can leave a chat. Whoever started it, and you as owner, can add or remove people and bots, rename it, archive it, or delete it. Only you can remove yourself. When someone leaves or is removed, bots stop the work they asked for. Deleting a chat also deletes its files. Archived chats sit under **Archived** below your group chats until you unarchive them. Chats you left, and ones people you invited started without you, are listed in **Settings → Members**, where you can join or delete them.
